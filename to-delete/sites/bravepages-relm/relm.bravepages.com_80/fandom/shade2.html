A Shade of Jealousy

Part Two

By Relm


“So Gourry what are your plans? Are you going to stay in town for a few days?” Sylphiel asked Gourry once they had finished eating lunch.

“I guess so. Maybe I could help out with the town at bit. Is there anything that I could do to help?”

“Well tomorrow I was going to help with some of the farming. Would you like to help me tend to the crops?” Sylphiel suggested.

“What kinds of crops do you have growing?”

“Oh lots of things! Carrots, tomatoes, strawberries, bell peppers, squash, rhubarb, pumpkins, peas... and a bunch more. We have so many because Wallace and his father brought lots of equipment and supplies to farm. Thanks to them we’re going to have lots of produce in the years to come.” Sylphiel beamed. She was really proud of her plants.

Gourry’s face darkened a bit at the mention of Wallace’s name. They had spent the whole time eating lunch talking about many things and that had been nice. But even saying Wallace’s name once and Sylphiel’s face lit up like a Christmas tree. ‘She must like that guy a lot. Did she ever like me like that? She always called me Gourry-dear. But she’s not doing that anymore...’ Gourry realized and now his mood got darker.

“Gourry? Is something wrong? Do you not want to help me tomorrow?” Sylphiel asked with worry evident in her voice. ‘Did I say something wrong? Is he upset?’

“Oh sorry! No nothing’s wrong. Just thinking about something... I would love to help you tomorrow. Does New Siaraag have an inn?”

“Yes we have an inn. But why do you ask?”

“Well if I’m going to help out for a while then I’ll need a place to stay.” Gourry rationalized.

“What? No you have to stay with me!” Sylphiel blurted out. As soon as she said she blushed. ‘That sounded so desperate! Why do I have no will when it comes to Gourry?’

“Are you sure Sylphiel? I don’t want to be a bother.” Gourry secret hoped that Sylphiel would decline her invitation. It’s not that he didn’t want to stay with Sylphiel, but he was leery of hearing her talk about Wallace.

“You aren’t a bother. And I don’t see why you’d have to go and spend money to stay at a place when you’d be doing the town a favour. Plus it would be nice to someone else around at home.” Sylphiel couldn’t help but thing about her father at that moment. She always tried to put on a brave face and a bright smile but the loneliness was very present no matter what she did.

Gourry saw the change in Sylphiel’s mood. “Alright Sylphiel I’ll stay. But I should warn you, I snore very loudly.”

“I remember.” Sylphiel giggled. “But you didn’t snore too badly. They say you snore when you are sleeping very well.”

“I guess I’m very good at sleeping then.” Gourry laughed.

Sylphiel laughed. “I think you’re probably the best at it out of everyone I know.”

Gourry smiled. ‘I may be a goof, but at least I can still make her smile.’ “So what were you planning on doing for the rest of the day before I bumped into you?”

“Well I was just going to show Wallace some of the places where I used to play as a child. And then we were going to relax by the lake.”

“Oh.” Gourry’s smile faded. “So I guess I ruined your date with Wallace.”

“No not at all! It wasn’t a date to begin with.” Sylphiel laughed nervously while blushing slightly. “We were just hanging out, that’s all.”

“While holding hands...” Gourry muttered.

“What was that Gourry? What did you say?”

“Um nothing... So do you think you could show me around town? I’d love to see what’s been built.”

“Of course! Let’s go!”

The rest of the day Sylphiel showed Gourry around New Siaraag. Originally Gourry had thought a lot had been done with the town, but he didn’t expect the things Sylphiel showed him.

Sure the town had the usual things, an inn, a couple restaurants, homes, and a shrine. But what he didn’t expect was the hospital, a school (both regular and magic), clothing and general stores, stables, a tavern, a blacksmith shop, and even a theatre! There wasn’t anything that Gourry could think of to add to the town that it didn’t already have. At present Sylphiel was showing Gourry the fields they would be tending.

“Wow, this place really is a full fledged town!” Gourry exclaimed in awe.

Sylphiel blushed. “Well we still need our crops to grow. And we’re going to expand the farm to have more livestock. We have chickens, pigs and goats already.”

“Well that stuff is growing pretty well already.” Gourry commented as he went to a thorny bush that had wrapped itself around a big boulder. Dark purple berries dotted the vines, shining in the fading daylight.

“Oh that’s a blackberry bush. Some people call it a weed which is one of the reasons why we have goats. They eat the thorny vines. But I love the berries the plant produces.” Sylphiel gently plucked one of the berries off the vine and popped into her mouth.

Gourry of course had to try these berries. But when he went to pluck one first he poked himself on some of the thorns, then squished the berry as he picked it. “Ewww.” Gourry looked at the dark purple mush on his hands. He didn’t dwell on it long, as he immediately licked the berry guts from his fingers. “That does taste good. But how can you pick it and not have it turn to mush?”

“The key is to be gentle as you pick them. Blackberries can get very soft and mushy as they ripen. Keep your hands as soft as you can and pick it very carefully.” Sylphiel demonstrated as she picked another berry. “See?” She placed the newly picked berry in Gourry’s hand.

Gourry popped the berry into his mouth. He went to try and pick another one but again he was being too rough with the berries.

“No, Gourry like this.” Sylphiel grabbed his hand and showed him the right way.

Though Gourry was wearing fingerless gloves he still almost recoiled from the feel of Sylphiel’s fingers on his own. It was then that he realized that Sylphiel was very close to him. Close enough that it wasn’t just their hands that were touching, but her whole body was flesh up against his as she stood behind him. Gourry almost blanked out there for a moment. ‘What’s going on? Why am I feeling like this?’ He mentally questioned himself. Sure in the past he had recognized Sylphiel as being a pretty girl, but now something just felt different. Like being so close to Sylphiel was nice but it felt very awkward.

Sylphiel caught on to this vibe too and scampered away from Gourry once he picked the berry. “We should pick some berries for desert tonight.” She tried to hide her face as she knew she was blushing. ‘A simple touch... and this is what happens to me.’ She berated herself.

“Oh yeah it’s starting to get dark.” Gourry noticed looking up to the sky. The sun was setting making the sky glow with hues of yellow, orange, pink and red.

“Well then let’s pick the berries quickly. Then we can get home and I can start making dinner.”

Wallace had been watching Gourry and Sylphiel for quite some time and he wasn’t amused. Though the two said nothing to hint at it, but Wallace knew there was something going on between the swordsman and the shrine maiden.

“Looks like you’ve got competition my boy!” Gorrich said to his son as he slapped on him the back. “Do you know who your new rival is?”

“That is Gourry Gaberieve, the so called swordsman of light.” Wallace answered bitterly.

“Master Gaberieve? Why I’ve heard of him! He’s not the brightest, but he is a well known hero around these parts. He’s supposed to be braver then most and have a pure heart. That’s going to be one tough guy to beat.”

Wallace rolled his eyes. “I don’t care if he slew Zanifar himself, he doesn’t stand a chance. There’s no way he could out charm me. Sylphiel already likes me quite a bit.” He smirked triumphantly.

“Well I hope for your sake, you’ve charmed her enough. Because if you haven’t you’ll surely loose.” Gorrich advised his son.

“What do you mean by that?”

“From what I’ve been told, dear Sylphiel has been holding a torch for the young master for quite some time. So he has no need to charm her, as she’s already smitten. But good luck to you anyways.” Gorrich waved goodbye as he left Wallace to mule over what he said.

‘Damn. Well you are a fool Gourry Gaberieve, to let such a sweet and fair lady pine after you while you ignore her isn’t wise thing to do. As the heart is a fickle thing, and the hearts of fair maidens aren’t meant to be wasted on fools.’

Dinner was nice as Gourry and Sylphiel ate talking and laughing. He didn’t know hanging around Sylphiel could be so much fun, or if he did then he had previously forgotten. Gourry talked about stories of his and Lina’s adventures and Sylphiel laughed at Gourry’s absent minded way of telling them. He kept forgetting pieces of what he was talking about getting lost mid story. Even forgot the adventures he was telling her about included her as well.

“Oh Gourry!” Sylphiel said in between laughs. “That memory of yours is something else.”

Gourry nervously rubbed the back of his head. “I don’t know why I keep forgetting things. I’m sorry.”

“It’s okay!” Sylphiel giggled. “That’s just part of who you are.” She started cleaning their plates. “You always know how to make me laugh, and that’s the most important thing.”

Gourry eyed the pie cooling on the counter. Sylphiel had made and baked a blackberry pie for them to have as desert. And after trying those blackberries previously before dinner Gourry was more than keen to try this pie.

Sylphiel laughed as she looked at Gourry. “Are you afraid it’s going to walk away?” She joked.

“Huh?” Gourry was broken from his thoughts.

Sylphiel pointed to the pie. “You’re staring down the pie pretty intently. Either you’re afraid it’s going to run away or disappear. Unless it has offended you in some way.”

Gourry laughed. “No it hasn’t offended me. Except it might be offended with me wanting to eat it.”

“Well the pie’s purpose is to be eaten after all. I doubt it would be offended.”

“But once you eat it it’s gone. Why would it not want to exist?”

Sylphiel laughed. “Well then Gourry should we leave it be and not eat it at all?”

Gourry’s face paled. After spending the whole dinner thinking about that pie, the mere mention of not eating make Gourry quiet upset. “No I didn’t say that!”

“Really I’m sure you were questioning its’ state of mind and whether or not it would like to be eaten. Are you changing your mind?” Sylphiel playfully threw a dishcloth at Gourry.

“Yeah I think it would like to be eaten. Can we eat it now?”

Sylphiel shook her head. “It still needs time to cool, I just took it out of the oven after all. I’m going to wash these dishes, why don’t you help me by drying them?”

“It’s the least I can do; you made all that delicious food after.”

“The greatest complement to any chef is a full stomach and an empty plate.” Sylphiel stated with a sagely voice.

“Then you must get more complements than any person in the world.”

Sylphiel blushed and laughed again. She started washing the dishes as quickly as possible, she didn’t want to keep Gourry from eating his pie.

Gourry was also quick to dry the dishes, he was also thinking about the pie. He was so fast that he almost dropped a plate or two. One slipped and almost fell the ground. Gourry was quick, he caught in time, but in doing so he dropped another one. And in going for that one he dropped another one. By the end of it Gourry was balancing plates all over while standing on one foot.

Sylphiel roared with laughter. “Gourry, perhaps you should go slower.” Sylphiel helped get the plates off Gourry.

“I didn’t break any though!” He said triumphantly.

“Yes you’re very talented. Seems like you’re used to juggling plates.”

Gourry laughed nervously. “I used to get in trouble when I broke plates.”

“Well we’re finished here, so let’s eat that pie.”

Gourry was positively giddy as they sat down to eat the pie.

The pie still steamed as Sylphiel cut into it. It was still a bit warm. “Gourry you may want to wait-“ Sylphiel started to say but Gourry just dived right in.

“OUCH! It’s hot!” Gourry stuck out his burnt tongue.

“How about I get some ice cream? That should cool the pie enough to eat.” Sylphiel suggested while getting some vanilla ice cream from her ice chest.

A couple dollops of ice cream and it was pie heaven.

Sylphiel only ate one piece; she was full from dinner after all. Gourry ate the rest of the pie. But she didn’t mind so much. She expected Gourry to eat the rest of it anyway. If was nice to have someone else to eat with. Living alone Sylphiel would make things and have lots of leftovers. She could usually find people in town willing to eat what she couldn’t but she was always worried that the food was going to waste.

“I’m sorry I ate it all.” Gourry mumbled embarrassedly.

“I was expecting you to so no worries.” Sylphiel assured him as she cleaned up their plates.

“Yeah I bet you were hoping to have leftovers so you could eat it in the next day for breakfast.”

Sylphiel laughed. “Pie for breakfast? Gourry that’s silly!”

“Pie for breakfast sounds delicious.” Gourry said out loud reminiscing on the pie he just ate.

“So if we had pie for breakfast, then I guess it would be cake for lunch, ice cream for dinner, but then what would be dessert?”

“More pie.”

Sylphiel roared with laughter. “So you’re so funny Gourry. How about I show you the room you will be sleeping in.”

Gourry laid in his bed trying to sleep but failing miserably. It’s not that he had a stomach ache from eating too much or that the bed was uncomfortable. He felt fine and in any other circumstances he would have been off to sleep easily on a bed like that. His mind was what was preventing him from sleeping. He kept going through the events of the day. He had so much fun spending time with Sylphiel. But it also bothered him. He didn’t like that guy Wallace and how friendly he was with Sylphiel. Gourry didn’t know why it bothered him.

‘Sylphiel deserves to be happy more than anyone I know.’ Gourry mentally mused rolling over on his other side in the bed. ‘So her dating someone shouldn’t be a bad thing. Because if she had a boyfriend she wouldn’t be alone. She would be happy and have a reason to smile. Instead of being so sad all the time.’

Gourry let out a heavy sigh as he stared into the darkness of the room. ‘But why does it have to be him?’
