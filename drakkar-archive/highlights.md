## 1. Brad Lineberger's Definitive Origin Story (September 2003)

**Source**: [Past Lives Part 2](https://landsofkes.com/archive/iok/miscellaneous/articles/kod_iok_discussion_2.htm)
**Posted by**: Brad (KoD Creator), September 14, 2003
**Significance**: The single most authoritative account of Drakkar's creation, posted by Brad himself.

### Key Excerpts

On creating the game:
> "I am the sole creator of Realm which became Drakkar, I wrote it in college."

On the original design:
> "Originally center-screen side scroller MMRPG like Rogue. [A] friend introduced him to IoK; adopted similar symbol set, moved display upper-right."

On the technical origins:
> "Developed on PDP11, ported to Unix."

On the MPGN era and internet firsts:
> "MPGN obtained early Class B internet address via C$S [CompuServe], put Drakkar online as 'original, first, and only graphical MMRPG on the internet.'"

On selling and buying back the game:
> "10 years prior [circa 1993], MPGN purchased complete rights from Brad. MPGN later merged with IEN; Brad consulted on other projects. Recently bought Drakkar back after it 'died on the vine.'"

On his IoK identity:
> Brad was **Soyer**, a legendary drake hunter in the CURE guild on Island of Kesmai. When asked directly, he declined to confirm, citing "protecting the innocent."

### Why This Matters
This post establishes: (1) Brad as sole creator, (2) Realm as the original name, (3) development on a PDP-11, (4) influence from Rogue and IoK, (5) the claim of being the first graphical MMRPG on the internet, (6) the timeline of rights transfers.

## 2. The "Massively Multiplayer" Term Origin Claim

**Source**: [The Game Archaeologist: Kingdom of Drakkar](https://massivelyop.com/2015/07/25/the-game-archaeologist-kingdom-of-drakkar/) (Massively Overpowered, July 2015)

> "The term 'massively multiplayer' was coined at MPG-Net's Key West office during a meeting between Drakkar and Legends of Kesmai teams to differentiate from eight-player games."

### Why This Matters
If verifiable, this places the coining of "massively multiplayer" at MPG-Net in Key West, Florida, in the early 1990s — before the term was widely adopted. This is a significant claim in MMORPG history.

## 3. Purgury's iEN-Era Server Crisis Documentation (1999-2000)

**Source**: [Purgury's Angelfire Page](https://wolfguild.com/mirrors/Purgury/www.angelfire.com/ok4/Drakkar/index.html) (mirrored on Wolf Guild)

This page is a primary source document from the iEntertainment Network era, capturing the community experience during server instability.

### Key Details
- Server experiencing "hardware and software problems during a move"
- Database confirmed intact; forums operational
- News server at `news.ient.com`
- Head SYSOP was **MoonShadow** — players were asked to stop messaging her
- Some system parts "were damaged during the move"
- Copyright notice: "©1999,2000 iENTERTAINMENT NETWORK"
- Creator listed guild affiliations: LadyDracmare (MIA guild), Purgury (MoY guild)

### Rare Item Gallery
The page documented items with screenshots that may no longer exist elsewhere:
- Def. Axe, Easter Robe, Def. Hally, Craymen defense hally
- Koss Mace, Def. Sword, Obsidian Dagger, Silver Short Sword
- Pally Gaunts, Dread Sword, SeaHag Robe, Hag Plate
- MoonBoots, Berovar Sword (intentionally no details given)

### Why This Matters
This is a snapshot of the community during the troubled iEN transition period (1999-2000), just before iEN collapsed and Brad bought the game back. The rare item screenshots may be the only surviving documentation of these items' appearance in the old graphics.

## 4. The BORED Guild Links Directory — A Rosetta Stone

**Source**: [BORED Guild Links Page](https://wolfguild.com/mirrors/BORED/www.geocities.com/telletran/Links.html) (mirrored on Wolf Guild, originally geocities.com/telletran)

This single page contained **40 outbound links** to other Drakkar fan sites and guilds, making it the most comprehensive directory of the pre-DrakkarZone community ever found. It revealed:

- **17 guild sites** not previously documented (BLOOD, KLRZ, BKHND, NLOE, DREAD, WILD, etc.)
- **5 Yahoo Clubs** for Drakkar guilds (all now dead — Yahoo shut down Clubs)
- **Multiple GeoCities neighborhoods** hosting Drakkar content
- **Cross-links** between GeoCities, Angelfire, Tripod, Xoom, NBCi, and other free hosts
- The **gamechatter.com/sas** Drakkar FAQ (previously unknown)
- **Emily's Forum** on Delphi (previously unknown community hub)

### Why This Matters
This is the most complete snapshot of the Drakkar web community circa 2001-2002. Many of these sites have never been documented anywhere else. Each URL is a potential Wayback Machine target.

## 5. Damon Slye (Villagers & Heroes) Endorsement

**Source**: [The Game Archaeologist](https://massivelyop.com/2015/07/25/the-game-archaeologist-kingdom-of-drakkar/)

> Damon Slye, creator of Villagers & Heroes (and Red Baron, Aces of the Pacific at Dynamix):
> "I played this game back in 1993 with a bunch of my colleagues at Dynamix. It was an excellent game. It was well balanced and a lot of fun."

### Why This Matters
A major game industry figure confirming he played Drakkar in 1993, establishing the game's reputation within the professional game development community during the MPG-Net era.

## 6. GameSpot Coverage of iMagic/GameHub Launch (April 2000)

**Source**: [IMagic Cranks Up MPG-Net](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/imagic-cranks-up-mpg-net/1100-2465873/) (GameSpot)

GameSpot reported the launch of GameHub by I-Magic (Interactive Magic):
- $4.95 monthly subscription
- Featured: Kingdom of Drakkar, Unreal, Total Annihilation, WarBirds
- Chat rooms, forums, online shopping

**Second article**: [MPG-Net Looks for Members](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/mpg-net-looks-for-members/1100-2462166/)

### Why This Matters
Contemporary mainstream gaming press coverage of Drakkar's platform. Places KoD alongside major commercial titles (Unreal, Total Annihilation) on the same service.

## 7. Coming Soon Magazine — MPG-Net Advertisement

**Source**: [csoon.com/issue30/p_mpgn1.htm](https://www.csoon.com/issue30/p_mpgn1.htm)

> "Prepare for Winter by taking advantage of the limited time FREE offer and start playing the exciting FRPG 'Kingdom of Drakkar'" at mpgn.com.

**E3 Atlanta 1997 coverage** ([csoon.com/issue27/e3_2.htm](https://www.csoon.com/issue27/e3_2.htm)):
MPG-NET announced at E3:
- **Empire Dawns** — real-time strategy (colony → galactic empire)
- **DragonGard** — first-person 3D 16-bit color engine, thousands of players
- **FiefQuest** — land acquisition, resource management, diplomacy

### Why This Matters
Rare period advertising and trade show coverage. Shows MPG-Net's ambitions beyond Drakkar, and positions KoD in the context of late-1990s gaming.

## 9. The Ironuzak Thread — First-Person History

**Source**: [Past Lives Part 2](https://landsofkes.com/archive/iok/miscellaneous/articles/kod_iok_discussion_2.htm)

A remarkable exchange where **Ironuzak** (real name Brem), a legendary IoK Martial Artist, responds to a thread discussing his own legend:

> "Ironuzak himself responds. Discusses Iron Lich boots possibly unintended in BG version. Notes he was briefly ICE guild member before leaving due to guild politics. Mentions invitation to join CURE in KoD (former IoK CURE members). References Mord as first/only Ninja (MA/Thief hybrid)."

And a player named **wardon** recalling 1981:
> "Last time I played was probably in May or June of 1981 (before wife and kids...) on Commodore 64 at $6/hour connection cost in Huntington Beach."

### Why This Matters
First-person accounts from the earliest era of online gaming. The 1981 date predates even Realm (1984), referring to Island of Kesmai on CompuServe — the direct ancestor/influence on Drakkar.
