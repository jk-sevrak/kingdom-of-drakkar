# Guilds & Community

Kingdom of Drakkar had a thriving guild system and player community. Guilds were the primary social organizing structure, predating modern tools like Discord. Guild websites served as community hubs, knowledge bases, and recruitment platforms.

## Guild Organization

Common features across Drakkar guilds:
- **Formal membership** with application processes and requirements
- **Member rosters** with character names, levels, and classes
- **Rules and conduct expectations** - behavioral standards for members
- **Guild meetings** with recorded minutes (EDGE held formal meetings, e.g., February 2001)
- **Guild treasury** - some guilds tracked finances (LoE had a cash management system)
- **Event calendars** - scheduled hunts, boss runs, and special events
- **Links pages** - connecting to other Drakkar resources and allied guilds

## Guild Tags

Characters could have guild tags as part of their names (e.g., "Paladium__NLOE"). When scrying another player, the guild tag had to be spelled exactly, including the tag.

Brad mentioned that the ability to change character names (including removing "lost" guild tag letters) existed but wasn't easy enough to make into a game feature. He hoped to offer it as a service again once "draktools gets ported."

## The Fan Site Ecosystem

### Web Hosting Platforms Used
The Drakkar community used virtually every major free hosting platform of the late 1990s/early 2000s:
- **GeoCities** (multiple neighborhoods: Area51, TimesSquare, SiliconValley)
- **Angelfire** (rpg, rpg2, dragon2, dragon3 sections)
- **Tripod**
- **8m.com**
- **GuildHappy** (specifically for guild sites)
- **EarthLink** (personal pages)
- **iWarp/WebMagic**
- **BigStarGraphics**
- **GameChatter**
- **Dedicated domains** (kingdomofdrakkar.com, drakkarmoy.com, lords-of-entropy.de, drakkarzone.com, tantheus.com)

### Types of Fan Sites
1. **Comprehensive guides** (Tantheus, EDGE, Stormwinds) - item databases, quest walkthroughs, maps, creature stats
2. **Guild portals** (MoY, LoE, ROAR) - member management, forums, recruitment
3. **Personal character pages** (GeoCities individuals) - character showcases, screenshots
4. **Specialized resources** (ROAR Items, MoY Hit Doc Calculator) - focused game tools
5. **Link directories** (Billy the Barb) - connecting the community

### Notable Fan Sites

**Tantheus' Drakkar Page** (tantheus.com)
- The most comprehensive single-source reference for game data
- Covered all game worlds, all equipment types, all quest walkthroughs
- Served as the unofficial wiki before game wikis existed
- Represented thousands of hours of community documentation

**Stormwind's Archives** (Tripod)
- Organized by game world (Aleria, Cobrahn, Nork)
- Contained area guides, quest walkthroughs (Mausoleum Quest), and general info

**DrakVision** (Angelfire)
- Fan resource/news site

**Zgwortz's Drak Page** (EarthLink)
- Nork maps with compass overlay
- Nork navigation guides

**Drakdude's Kingdom of Drakkar** (GeoCities)
- "War Room" with critter combat data
- Nork loot list

## Community Culture

### Communication
- **In-game chat**: Shouts, talk, private messages (all in black text - players wanted colored text)
- **Forums**: Brad planned to consolidate to a single forum (considering EZBoard)
- **Conference sessions**: Q&A events with the developer
- **Friends list**: In-game, but lacked organizational features (no categories for guild members, etc.)
- **Ignore functionality**: Brad planned to add `/ignore` as a slash command

### Social Norms
- **"Ask, do not beg"** - Experienced players were willing to help newbies with basic gear if asked politely, but begging was frowned upon
- **FindFriendly etiquette**: Don't summon creatures in the Steel Flower or near newbies who might be killed
- **Party etiquette**: Sticks stay close, healers heal, ments provide protections. Players who ignored party needs weren't invited back.
- **PKing**: Strongly discouraged. Report to sysops rather than retaliating.

### Events
- **Christmas events**: "Christmas in Drakkar," "Christmas Special," "Christmas Present from Drakkar" - holiday-themed in-game events
- **Arcane events**: "Arcane Experience Boost," "Arcane and Party Health Boosts," "Arcane Cobrahn Special Event"
- **Coin Events**: Held at specific locations (e.g., Hagall)
- **Halloween events**: Had special NPCs that could change character model appearance
- **Real-life meetups**: Some guilds (LoE) organized in-person gatherings

### International Community
- Drakkar had a **global player base** despite being an American game
- **German-speaking European community** was particularly organized (LoE with .de domain, German CMS)
- **Timezone differences** meant European guilds had their own peak hours
- The game's text-based chat worked across language barriers to some extent

## Historical Significance

The Drakkar fan site ecosystem is a remarkable snapshot of late 1990s/early 2000s gaming web culture:
- Hand-coded HTML pages with period-typical design (animated GIFs, colored backgrounds, midi music)
- Free hosting platforms as the primary means of web presence
- Guild websites serving the role that Discord servers fill today
- Player-created databases filling the role that modern game wikis serve
- Link exchanges and web rings connecting disparate fan sites
- The transition from individual character pages to organized guild portals
- An era when "knowing someone with a website" was genuinely valuable in the gaming community
