# Kingdom of Drakkar: Overview

Kingdom of Drakkar (KoD) is a graphical MMORPG that has been running since **1989**, making it one of the longest-running online multiplayer RPGs in history. It bills itself as "the #1 online multiplayer role-playing game since 1989."

## What Is It?

Drakkar is a **top-down view graphical role-playing game** where players create characters, explore persistent worlds, fight creatures, complete quests, and interact with other players in real time. It emerged during the transition era from text-based MUDs to graphical MMORPGs, and was originally hosted on **MPG-Net (Multi-Player Games Network)**.

## Developer

The game was created and maintained by **Brad Burton** (burton@drakkarzone.com). The developer's domain was **DrakkarZone.com**, separate from the marketing/community site at kingdomofdrakkar.com. Brad was accessible to the community, holding Q&A sessions with players (a notable one took place on April 10, 2002).

In the 2002 Q&A, Brad described his vision:

> "My view of drakkar now is a product i want to expand out into the future, so i'm currently looking for ways to 'blow the top' off the game, giving me open level and skill expandability. This includes larger maps, new scenarios, more experience and skill levels, and more items which can affect more of your character. (and of course nasty creatures guarding them)"

## Platforms

- **Windows** (primary client)
- **Android** (client released later)
- **Web browser** (referenced as in development)

## History & Context

- **1989**: Game launched, originally on MPG-Net
- **Early 2000s**: Migrated to DrakkarZone (DZ), an independent hosting platform
- **2002**: Developer Q&A revealed plans for "Grimoire," a new scenario for characters level 23-25+
- **Ongoing**: The game received continuous updates including new lairs, items, events, and balance changes

Drakkar was contemporary with the "golden age" of MMORPGs (EverQuest, Dark Age of Camelot, etc.) and had players who moved between these games. The Lords of Entropy guild, for example, played both Drakkar and DAoC simultaneously.

## Game Worlds

The game features multiple distinct worlds with increasing difficulty:

1. **Nork** - The starting world where all players begin. Contains the town of Nork, the Frore ice area, Homlet, Carrena, Volcano Town, and numerous lairs/dungeons.
2. **Cobrahn** (Cob) - An advanced world requiring level 18+ to access. Contains Tree Town, Timmy Town, Doom Town, Desert Town, Seldar, and more challenging content.
3. **Aleria** - Another advanced world with its own unique content and challenges.
4. **Mjorin** (Land of Mjorin) - An additional exploration area, less commonly documented.

There were also "alt" worlds (alternate versions of the main game segments).

## Core Gameplay Loop

1. Create a character (choose gender, race, class, allocate stats)
2. Explore dungeons and wilderness areas
3. Fight creatures for experience, skill, and loot
4. Buy/sell/appraise items at NPC vendors
5. Train at trainers to improve skills
6. Max out health and energy points via Hit Doctor and EP pots
7. Form parties for group content
8. Complete quests for rewards and access to new areas
9. Progress to harder worlds (Nork -> Cobrahn -> Aleria)

## Key Game Philosophy

From the developer Q&A:

- **Skill over level**: "Do not fall for the trap of concentrating on your level! Skill is what matters in Drakkar. Level is secondary."
- **Group-oriented**: "This is a group oriented gaming, and the sum of a group is more than the sum of its parts."
- **1% per hour minimum**: Brad targeted a minimum of 1% skill gain per hour at higher levels with efficient hunting.
- **No 'winning'**: "There is no 'winning' the game in the traditional sense." Players set their own goals.

## Fan Community

The Drakkar community was remarkably active in creating fan sites across every major web hosting platform of the late 1990s/early 2000s:
- GeoCities, Angelfire, Tripod, 8m.com, GuildHappy, EarthLink
- Dedicated domains (drakkarmoy.com, lords-of-entropy.de)
- Fan sites served as the unofficial wikis before game wikis became standard
- Player-created databases documented items, creatures, maps, and strategies

The game had a truly international player base, with a significant German-speaking European community (the Lords of Entropy guild had its own .de domain and German-language CMS).
