## Forum Posts

### Original Post
A user reminisces about playing Classic Kingdom of Drakkar, sharing their personal
experience with the game. Key points:

- They played Classic Kingdom of Drakkar, believed to be around 1989.
- It cost $6 an hour to play, and their monthly budget went fast.
- They felt it probably had the best community of any game they had ever played in.
- It used to be text-based, sending players an ASCII map, and they eventually made
  an 8-bit GUI interface to go over the top.
- Kingdom of Drakkar probably borrowed from a lot of MUDs, and there was another
  graphic game out at the time called Kesmai.

### Community Responses

- Another commenter noted they played it around 1980 and it hasn't changed since
  then, calling it "a kind of museum piece."

- Players were charged by the hour rather than by the month, and for most people
  who played it, it was the first and only MMORPG they had ever experienced, so
  it seemed "miraculously powerful."

- Another poster observed that every old game with multiplayer had amazing
  communities because back then gaming wasn't as popular, and people actually had
  to deal with the consequences of their actions.

- One user recalled playing it on MPGnet via telnet, where it cost about $2 an hour.

## Summary
The thread highlights the historical significance of Kingdom of Drakkar as an early
online multiplayer RPG experience, predating many of the modern MMORPGs. The
discussion captures deep nostalgia and emphasizes the game's pioneering role in the
MMO genre, its text-based origins, the hourly billing model of early online gaming,
and the strong community bonds that formed among early players.
